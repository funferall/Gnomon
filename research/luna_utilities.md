# Massachusetts municipal utility GIS: hydrants, catch basins, manholes

Research snapshot: 2026-11-04. Sources below are official municipal pages or official municipal ArcGIS services. I did not find a verified Wellesley public asset download in this pass; its town storm-drain guidance is not an inventory. Cambridge is the strongest verified source and should be the first ground-truth candidate.

## Best verified source: Cambridge GIS

Cambridge maintains a GIS data dictionary with dataset descriptions, downloadable Shapefile/File Geodatabase, ArcGIS Online, and GitHub GeoJSON links. It states that the city has 200+ layers/formats. Each dataset page links the actual assets. Dictionary landing/download options: https://www.cambridgema.gov/gis/gisdata and https://www.cambridgema.gov/gis/gisdatadictionary .

### Counts

I did not verify exact feature counts from query responses; do not report a count based on map symbols or a service record cap. Cambridge Fire Department's official January 2026 post says there are nearly 2,000 hydrants: https://www.cambridgema.gov/news/detail?path=%2Fsitecore%2Fcontent%2Fhome%2Fcfd%2Fnews%2F2026%2F01%2Fhydrantshovelinginprogress . The operational service supports queries, so exact counts can be calculated by layer and lifecycle/owner filter from the layer's `returnCountOnly=true` endpoint.

## Wellesley

Official [Wellesley storm-drain marking guide](https://wellesleyma.gov/DocumentCenter/View/22076/GUIDE-TO-STORM-DRAIN-MARKING---Final2) distinguishes catch basins and drain manholes and explains stormwater is separate from sanitary sewer. It is educational material, not GIS inventory or download. Searches of official Wellesley sources in this pass did not locate a public hydrant/catch-basin/manhole feature service or bulk GIS download. Therefore public-map visibility should not be assumed to mean export access; inventory availability, update date, completeness, and license remain unknown. Treat Wellesley as unresolved, not absent.

## Other municipal leads checked

- **Boston/BWSC:** Official Boston construction plan documents demonstrate separate sewer manhole (SMH), drainage manhole (DMH), water manhole, catch basin, and hydrant categories (example official legend: https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2025/05/20250416TOWER1_NOI_Combined.pdf). These are project plans, not a citywide GIS inventory. No authoritative downloadable citywide municipal point layer verified here. License/count/current asset status unknown.
- **Somerville / Arlington / Belmont hydrants:** Cambridge's official PublicEye MapServer contains “Somerville Fire Hydrants” (layer 2), “Arlington Fire Hydrants” (3), “Belmont Fire Hydrants” (4), and Cambridge (1): https://gisserver.cambridgema.gov/arcgis/rest/services/PublicEye/MapServer . This is a useful neighboring-town discovery map, but the service is hosted by Cambridge, has no discovered metadata describing source dates/completeness/license, and is not sufficient ground truth for those towns. Queryable viewer/service, not verified bulk dataset.
- **MassGIS:** The official MassGIS detailed basemap lists public facilities (fire stations etc.), not municipal hydrants, catch basins, or utility manholes. No statewide municipal asset inventory verified. Statewide coverage should not be inferred from basemap availability.

## Ground-truth ranking and handling

1. Cambridge INFRA_Hydrants for active municipal-owned hydrants: explicit active/abandoned/proposed statuses and owner fields, official current metadata, bulk downloadable formats, and stated open license. Cross-check against Cambridge Fire/Water Department maintenance context.
2. Cambridge INFRA_Catchbasin for catch basins: detailed ownership, lifecycle, maintenance dates, and downloadable files. Treat record-level `LASTEDITDATE`/`LASTCLEANDATE` as observations/actions; page-level Last Modified is metadata publication/update.
3. Cambridge Cartegraph DPW operational layers for manholes and storm/sewer separation: strong classification semantics including abandoned and storm/sanitary/combined. Use as service-layer ground truth only with the caveat that no public license/download status was found.
4. Wellesley, Boston, Somerville and adjacent communities remain leads requiring a town-owned inventory/officially linked feature service and explicit metadata before claiming asset completeness. Separate sewer/drain manholes from water/electric/telecom covers, and distinguish public/active from private/abandoned using owner and lifecycle fields when provided.

### Boston official hydrant services (additional verified lead)

Boston exposes a queryable hydrant layer in its official GIS portal: [PublicSafety/OpenData MapServer layer 0](https://gisportal.boston.gov/ArcGIS/rest/services/PublicSafety/OpenData/MapServer/0), and a second PWD Cartegraph read-only FeatureServer layer 3 at https://gis.boston.gov/arcgis/rest/services/PWD/Cartegraph_PWD_readonly/FeatureServer/3. The public-safety layer includes ENABLED, PLACEMENT_ (date), UPDATE_DAT, FACILITY_I, INSTALL_DA, OWNER_CODE, location, manufacturer/model and coordinates; thus date and ownership/status clues exist, although field aliases are truncated and domain meanings need checking before filtering. The service is publicly queryable; I found no bulk export link or explicit license, and no exact count verified. No dataset-level metadata date surfaced; service crawl time is not a data update date. This is a stronger Boston hydrant source than project plans, but still confirm current active/public ownership via City/BWSC asset stewardship. The PWD Cartegraph hydrant service may overlap the PublicSafety layer; treat them as likely duplicate representations until IDs/counts and lineage are compared.
